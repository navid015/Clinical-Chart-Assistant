## Chief complaint
Shortness of breath and leg swelling for 3 days.

## History of present illness
70-year-old man with ischemic cardiomyopathy (LVEF 35% in 2023), CAD with LAD stent in 2019, and paroxysmal atrial fibrillation on apixaban. Three days of worsening dyspnea on exertion, two-pillow orthopnea, and a 4 kg weight gain after several high-sodium meals at a family event. Reports taking all medications. No chest pain. Allergic to sulfonamide antibiotics (rash in 2012); has taken furosemide since 2019 without any reaction.

## Examination
BP 152/94, HR 108 irregularly irregular, RR 24, SpO2 89% on room air, weight 93.5 kg. Jugular venous distension to the angle of the jaw. Bilateral crackles to mid lung fields. 2+ pitting edema to the knees.

## Data
ECG: atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response, no acute ST changes. High-sensitivity troponin flat at 22 and 24 ng/L. NT-proBNP 6840 pg/mL. Creatinine 1.2, sodium 134, potassium 4.1. Chest X-ray: pulmonary vascular congestion and small bilateral pleural effusions.

## Assessment and plan
Acute on chronic systolic heart failure exacerbation, likely triggered by dietary sodium and atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response.
- IV furosemide 40 mg twice daily; strict intake and output, daily weights.
- Supplemental oxygen to keep SpO2 above 92%.
- Rate control with metoprolol; continue apixaban.
- Transthoracic echocardiogram.
- Hold spironolactone until renal function trend is clear.
